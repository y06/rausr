## What it is

Posters and out-of-home formats place a public message in a physical environment. They range from cultural posters viewed at walking speed to large roadside boards seen for only a few seconds.

## Design for the viewing condition

The format alone does not define the problem. Distance, speed, lighting, placement, dwell time, and competing visual noise determine how much information can be understood.

### Hierarchy

Most successful outdoor pieces have one dominant entry point, one supporting idea, and a clear source or action. Additional details must earn their place. If every element asks for attention, none receives it.

### Scale and contrast

Artwork should be evaluated at an approximation of the intended viewing distance. Small desktop previews encourage detail that disappears in context. Strong figure–ground separation and generous spacing usually outperform delicate distinctions.

### Campaign consistency

A family of placements needs a repeated code: composition, colour, type, image treatment, character, or verbal device. The code creates recognition while individual executions keep the campaign fresh.

## Practical checks

- Can the main message be identified in two or three seconds?
- Is the advertiser or organiser unmistakable?
- Does the design work in the worst likely light and location?
- Are critical details legal, legible, and placed inside safe production areas?
- Can the idea adapt to multiple aspect ratios without losing its hierarchy?

## Common mistake

Treating a billboard as an enlarged leaflet. Increasing the dimensions does not increase attention time. Outdoor design usually becomes stronger as the number of simultaneous messages decreases.
