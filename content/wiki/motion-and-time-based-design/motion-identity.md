## What it is

Motion identity translates brand character into behaviour. It defines how an identity appears, changes, responds, and leaves the screen over time. Useful systems describe principles—such as direct, elastic, precise, layered, or calm—and show how those principles affect recurring components.

A single animated logo can be memorable, but it cannot answer every motion question. A complete system also guides everyday transitions, typography, imagery, interface feedback, video endings, and quiet moments when nothing should move.

## Components of the system

### Movement principles

Principles connect strategy to execution. They guide direction, acceleration, rhythm, transformation, and the relationship between elements without prescribing one identical animation everywhere. Describe each principle in plain language and pair it with a visible example. “Confident” is too vague until the team can show how a confident transition behaves.

Define spatial logic as well. Does content enter from its source, unfold from a grid, follow a directional path, or dissolve into the next scene? Consistent logic makes a family recognisable even when individual assets differ.

### Timing and easing

Duration changes meaning. Fast movement can feel responsive or urgent; slow movement can feel calm or obstructive. Easing communicates weight and intention, while linear movement is better suited to continuous or measured processes. The right timing depends on whether someone is watching a film, navigating an interface, or waiting for a task to complete.

Define a small range of timings for common purposes rather than one universal speed. Short interface feedback should acknowledge an action promptly. A campaign sequence can afford more expression. Check that the action remains clear if a viewer sees only part of the sequence.

### Brand assets in time

Logos, type, colour fields, crops, patterns, characters, and sound can enter, transform, or respond consistently. Type might reveal one line at a time, a symbol might assemble from a repeated gesture, or an image crop might lead a transition. The system should define restrained everyday behaviour as well as expressive campaign moments.

Sound can reinforce recognition, but the visual sequence must still communicate when muted. Conversely, important information should not depend on visuals alone when an audio alternative is needed.

## Build a usable motion toolkit

Start with a few representative applications: a short logo ending, a title card, a social-video opening, a product transition, and a reduced-motion alternative. Test the principles across different aspect ratios and lengths. A rule that works only in a widescreen brand film is not yet a system.

Document templates, editable files, timing ranges, easing curves, safe areas, audio guidance, and export settings. Include “do” and “avoid” examples with reasons. Production teams need enough freedom to solve new problems without reinventing the identity or copying one animation mechanically.

## Motion with a purpose

Movement can guide attention, explain cause and effect, show continuity, or add character. Ask which of these jobs each animation performs. If removing it improves understanding or speed, it may be decoration rather than communication.

For interfaces, define how the system behaves when animations are reduced or disabled. Functional state changes must remain visible. For campaigns, review the first and final frames, the silent version, and the moment a viewer might join halfway through.

## Practical checks

- Test silent viewing and sound-on viewing.
- Define reduced-motion alternatives for non-essential movement.
- Check that functional transitions clarify state rather than delaying it.
- Create templates for common ratios, durations, and production teams.
- Judge recognition across a sequence, not only on the final frame.

## Common mistake

Calling a single logo reveal a motion identity. A reveal is one asset. A motion identity is a set of reusable decisions that can produce many coherent assets across products, campaigns, presentations, and film without losing the brand's character.
