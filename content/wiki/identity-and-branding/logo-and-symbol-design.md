## What it is

A logo is a repeatable visual identifier. It may be a wordmark, lettermark, symbol, emblem, or a combination of these forms. Its main job is recognition: helping an audience connect many encounters with the same source.

A symbol becomes useful through consistent use and accumulated meaning. The drawing matters, but the contexts around it—products, communication, service, reputation, and repetition—give it significance.

## Core design questions

- **Distinctiveness:** can the mark be recognised among relevant competitors?
- **Legibility:** does it remain clear at the sizes and distances where it will actually appear?
- **Reproducibility:** can it survive one colour, poor printing, embroidery, small screens, and unusual materials?
- **Appropriateness:** does its tone fit the organisation and intended audience without relying on a cliché?
- **System fit:** can it work with type, colour, imagery, motion, and layout rather than competing with them?

## Typical process

### Define the recognition problem

Clarify the name, audience, positioning, competitive environment, and required applications. Audit existing marks in the category to find visual conventions and crowded territory.

### Explore concepts before polish

Develop several conceptual routes in simple form. Early sketches should test structure and meaning, not effects. Promising routes are reduced, combined, and redrawn.

### Test in context

A mark that looks convincing in isolation may fail on a phone header, building sign, document footer, social avatar, or product label. Test the smallest and most difficult uses early.

### Build the asset family

Prepare responsive variants, spacing rules, minimum sizes, colour versions, monochrome artwork, and accessible digital assets. Record these decisions in the identity guidelines.

## Common mistake

Trying to make the logo explain everything usually produces a complicated illustration. A useful mark is often simple enough to recognise quickly while the rest of the identity carries detail, tone, and storytelling.
