## What it is

Long-form typography is the design of text intended to be read over many paragraphs, pages, or screens. Unlike display typography, it is judged less by a single striking moment and more by the quality of the complete reading experience.

## The reading system

### Body text

The typeface, size, line length, leading, paragraph treatment, and contrast work as one system. Changing one variable affects the others. A wider measure generally needs more leading; a smaller size needs careful testing on the actual substrate or screen.

### Hierarchy

Headings, introductions, captions, quotations, notes, folios, tables, and navigation need distinct roles. Differences should be noticeable enough to scan but limited enough that the publication still feels coherent.

### Rhythm

Repeated spacing and alignment create pace. Baseline grids can coordinate columns and facing pages, but optical judgment still matters when headings, images, tables, and pull quotes interrupt the flow.

## Practical checks

- Test full paragraphs, not only specimen words.
- Review difficult content: long headings, numbers, URLs, footnotes, tables, and multiple languages.
- Check rivers, widows, orphans, and uneven colour in justified text.
- Print physical proofs for print work and test reflow at narrow widths for digital work.
- Confirm that hierarchy remains understandable without relying on colour alone.

## Common mistake

Choosing body type by personality before testing readability. A typeface may look appropriate in a heading and still become tiring across forty pages. The correct test is the real content, at final size, in its final medium.
